Kylian Mbappé Biography: Age, Career, Net Worth, Records & Personal Life

He won a World Cup at 19, became Paris Saint-Germain's all-time top scorer, and then did what he had dreamed of since childhood: he joined Real Madrid — and in his very first season there, he won the European Golden Shoe. In 2026 he went further still, winning a second World Cup Golden Boot and becoming the all-time top scorer in World Cup history. Now, as captain of France, Kylian Mbappé is the most searched footballer in Europe — and the richest name on this site.

Who Is Kylian Mbappé?

Kylian Mbappé is a French footballer who plays as a forward for Real Madrid and captains the France national team. He is widely regarded as one of the best players of his generation — not only for his speed and finishing, but for how early he reached the top: a World Cup winner at 19, a club's all-time top scorer by 25, and a European Golden Shoe winner in his first season at Real Madrid.

What makes his story different from most football biographies is the speed of it. There was no long wait for a breakthrough. At 16 he was playing senior football for Monaco. At 18 he was the most expensive teenager in history. At 19 he had a World Cup winners' medal. At 27 he holds the record no one else in the history of the tournament has held: the most goals ever scored at World Cups. And at 25, after seven seasons at Paris Saint-Germain, he left on a free transfer to fulfil the childhood dream he had talked about for years: playing for Real Madrid.

Age and Early Life — Paris to Bondy

Kylian Mbappé Lottin was born on 20 December 1998 in Paris, and raised in Bondy, a suburb north-east of the capital. Football was the family business: his father, Wilfried Mbappé, born in Cameroon, was a football coach and later his agent; his mother, Fayza Lamari, born in Algeria to a Kabyle family, played handball at a high level and also works as his agent. His younger brother, Ethan, also became a professional footballer and now plays for Lille, while his older adoptive brother, Jirès Kembo Ekoko, played professionally too.

Bondy shaped him. He started playing for the local club AS Bondy at the age of six, with his father among his coaches, before joining the famous Clairefontaine academy — the French national football centre — and then Monaco's academy at 14. As a boy he supported Real Madrid, and at 11 he was invited to train with the club's under-12 side. He started learning Spanish at 15 and eventually became fluent. Posters of Cristiano Ronaldo, his idol, were on his bedroom wall. Years later, when he was presented at the Santiago Bernabéu, he would say that joining Real Madrid was a dream he had carried since he was a child.

Monaco — The Teenage Breakthrough

Mbappé made his senior debut for Monaco on 2 December 2015, aged 16, becoming the club's youngest-ever first-team player, and scored his first goal in February 2016. In the 2016–17 season he became a first-team regular, scoring 26 goals in 44 matches as Monaco won the Ligue 1 title — the club's first in 17 years — and reached the semi-finals of the Champions League, beating Manchester City and Borussia Dortmund on the way. Mbappé scored six goals in that Champions League run, and finished the season as one of the most wanted teenagers in world football.

That summer, Paris Saint-Germain signed him — first on loan in 2017, then permanently in 2018 for €180 million. It made him the second-most expensive player in history at the time, and the most expensive teenager ever. He was 18.

Paris Saint-Germain — All-Time Top Scorer

At PSG, Mbappé spent seven seasons, from 2017 to 2024, and became the club's all-time top scorer with 256 goals in 308 games. He won 15 trophies with the club, including six Ligue 1 titles and four Coupe de France titles, was named Ligue 1 Player of the Year five times — a record — and was the league's top scorer in six consecutive seasons, from 2018–19 to 2023–24, also a record. In 2021–22 he became the first player ever to finish a Ligue 1 season as both its top scorer and its top assist provider. His partnership with Neymar and, later, Lionel Messi made PSG one of the most watched teams in the world — but the Champions League, the trophy the club wanted most, never came. PSG reached the final in 2020 and lost to Bayern Munich.

He left in the summer of 2024, when his contract expired. The departure was not quiet: a dispute over unpaid wages and bonuses went to a Paris labour court, which ruled in December 2025 that PSG should pay him up to €61 million. PSG said in February 2026 that it would not appeal. The court case is a footnote to his football story, but it is a reminder that even the biggest transfers in football are also business.

"I always said Real Madrid was my dream. Now it is real."— Mbappé, at his Real Madrid presentation, July 2024

France — World Cup Winner at 19, Captain at 24

Mbappé won the 2016 European Under-19 Championship with France, scoring five goals, and made his senior debut in March 2017, aged 18, scoring his first international goal that August against the Netherlands. A year later, at the 2018 World Cup in Russia, he became the youngest French player ever to score at a World Cup, scored twice against Argentina in the round of 16, and then scored in the final against Croatia as France won 4–2. He was only the second teenager in history to score in a World Cup final, after Pelé in 1958, and he was named the tournament's Best Young Player.

He scored the winning goal as France beat Spain in the 2021 Nations League final. At Euro 2020 he missed the decisive penalty in the shoot-out defeat to Switzerland, and at Euro 2024 France reached the semi-finals but lost to Spain. Four years after his first World Cup, at the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, he went further as a goalscorer — and further, in the end, as a loser. He won the Golden Boot with 8 goals, and scored a hat-trick in the final against Argentina, the first hat-trick in a World Cup final since Geoff Hurst in 1966. France still lost, on penalties, after a 3–3 draw. It remains the most famous final of his career, and the one he most wants to forget.

In March 2023, after Hugo Lloris retired from international football, Mbappé was named captain of France, aged 24 — and scored twice in his first match wearing the armband. By September 2026 he had scored 67 goals in 107 appearances for France, making him France's all-time top scorer.

World Cup 2026 — Golden Boot Again, and the All-Time Record

The 2026 World Cup, played in Canada, Mexico and the United States, gave Mbappé the record that defines his international career. He scored twice in the opening 3–1 win over Senegal, and scored again against Iraq, Sweden, Paraguay and Morocco as France reached the semi-finals, where they lost 2–0 to Spain. In the third-place match France lost 6–4 to England — but Mbappé scored twice in that game, taking his tournament tally to 10 goals and 4 assists in 8 matches.

Those 10 goals won him the Golden Boot, making him the first player ever to win the award at two World Cups — and the first to reach double figures in a single tournament since Gerd Müller scored 10 in 1970. He was also named the Bronze Ball winner as the tournament's third-best player. And the two goals against England took his career World Cup total to 22 goals in 22 appearances — the most any player has ever scored at World Cups, ahead of Lionel Messi on 21 and Miroslav Klose on 16. France finished fourth, without a medal, but Mbappé left the tournament with the two records that matter most to a striker: the Golden Boot, and the all-time scoring record.

Real Madrid — The Childhood Dream, and the Golden Shoe

Real Madrid announced on 3 June 2024 that Mbappé had agreed to join the club for the following five seasons, on a free transfer. He was presented at a packed Santiago Bernabéu on 16 July 2024, in front of 80,000 fans. He scored on his debut, in the UEFA Super Cup in August 2024, and again in the FIFA Intercontinental Cup final in December 2024 — Madrid won both trophies.

His first La Liga season was, by the numbers, the best debut season any Real Madrid player has ever had. He scored 31 league goals in 34 games, winning the Pichichi Trophy as La Liga's top scorer and the European Golden Shoe as Europe's top scorer — his first time winning either award, and the first Golden Shoe for a Real Madrid player since Cristiano Ronaldo in 2015. He wore the #9 shirt that season; for 2025–26 he took the #10 shirt, after Luka Modrić left the club.

In 2025–26 he won a second Pichichi with 25 league goals and finished as the Champions League's top scorer with 15, scoring 42 goals in 44 appearances for Madrid across all competitions — but the team won no major trophy. Even so, his year was recognised at the Ballon d'Or: at the 70th ceremony, held in London on 26 October 2026, he finished runner-up behind Harry Kane, with Lamine Yamal third — the closest Ballon d'Or vote in years, decided by a narrow margin.

The 2026–27 season started the same way. A hat-trick against Real Sociedad in August, a goal against Inter Milan that took him to 71 Champions League goals, and on 25 October his 100th goal for the club, in a 2–1 El Clásico win at Barcelona. As of 7 November 2026 he had scored 103 goals in 119 games for Real Madrid.

Playing Style — What Makes Mbappé Special?

Mbappé's style of play combines speed, technique, intelligence, and confidence. He can operate as a central striker or from the left side of the attack, giving his team different options — and early in his career he was often compared to Thierry Henry and Pelé.

His biggest strengths are easy to name: exceptional speed — he can accelerate quickly and leave defenders behind; finishing — he is comfortable scoring with either foot and can finish from different angles; dribbling — his close control helps him beat defenders in one-on-one situations; movement — he makes intelligent runs into open spaces and creates chances for himself and teammates; and big-game performances — his World Cup record, 22 goals in 22 appearances, shows his ability to perform on the biggest occasions. Together, they have made him one of the most dangerous attacking players in world football.

Awards and Records

Mbappé's honours list is already long, and he is still 27. His major team honours are: the 2016 UEFA European Under-19 Championship with France; the Ligue 1 title with Monaco in 2016–17; the FIFA World Cup with France in 2018, and a runners-up medal in 2022; the UEFA Nations League with France in 2021; six Ligue 1 titles with Paris Saint-Germain; and the UEFA Super Cup and FIFA Intercontinental Cup with Real Madrid in 2024.

His individual honours include: FIFA World Cup Best Young Player 2018; Golden Boy 2017; Kopa Trophy 2018, as the best player under 21 in the world; FIFA World Cup Golden Boot 2022 and 2026; runner-up for The Best FIFA Men's Player in 2022; the Gerd Müller Trophy 2024; the European Golden Shoe 2024–25; the Pichichi Trophy as La Liga's top scorer in 2024–25 and 2025–26; Champions League top scorer in 2023–24 (shared) and 2025–26; Ballon d'Or runner-up 2026; Ligue 1 Player of the Year five times; a place in the FIFPRO World 11 six times; and a place on Time magazine's list of the 100 most influential people in 2023.

The records tell the same story in numbers: the most goals ever scored at World Cups (22); the most goals ever scored in World Cup finals (4); the first player to win the World Cup Golden Boot twice; 10 goals in World Cup knockout games; the all-time top scorer for both Paris Saint-Germain (256 goals) and the France national team; the most expensive teenager in football history (€180 million, 2018); the second teenager after Pelé to score in a World Cup final; and the first player to score a hat-trick in a World Cup final in 56 years.

Business, Brands and Life Off the Pitch

Mbappé signed his first sponsorship deal as a child — with Nike, when he was eight years old — and wore the brand for almost twenty years. That ended in September 2026, when he signed with Swiss sportswear company On: a ten-year agreement that includes an equity stake in the company, as On enters football for the first time, with Thierry Henry as its Director of Football. On's first football boots are due in 2027. He is also an ambassador for Hublot and Dior, and has a partnership with Oakley.

Beyond endorsements he is building a business of his own. He founded the production company Zebra Valley in 2022, holds stakes in companies including Sorare, Loewe and Alan through his investment business, and in 2024 became the majority owner of Ligue 2 club SM Caen. He has donated his World Cup prize money to charity and supports sports development projects in Africa, and he set up the Inspired by KM foundation to help children from disadvantaged backgrounds.

Net Worth 2026

Kylian Mbappé's net worth is estimated at between $180 million and $250 million. That range is the honest answer, and it needs explaining. No audited figure for his wealth exists — he has never published his finances. Different trackers publish very different numbers: Celebrity Net Worth puts the figure at $250 million (snapshot 4 November 2026), while other trackers range from about $180 million at the low end to $300 million at the high end, depending on how they count his signing bonus, endorsements and investments.

What is clear is where the money comes from. His Real Madrid salary is reported at around €31 million gross a year, with a signing bonus reported at between €100 million and €150 million, paid in instalments over his five-year contract. His endorsements now include On, Hublot and Dior, and Forbes put his total earnings in 2026 at about $95 million — around $70 million from football and $25 million from endorsements and other ventures. Personal Life

Mbappé keeps his private life out of the press, and this article does the same. He is not married, and he does not talk publicly about relationships — online speculation about his romantic life should not be treated as confirmed information. What he does talk about is charity: he set up the Inspired by KM foundation to help children from disadvantaged backgrounds, and he is reported to have donated his earnings from the 2018 World Cup to charity. He is known for his image-rights stance — he has refused to take part in French national team photoshoots with sponsors he does not endorse, because he wants control over how his image is used.
