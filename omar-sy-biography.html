Who Is Omar Sy?

Omar Sy is a French actor whose career is, in the simplest terms, a climb in three acts: first a beloved comedy double-act on French television, then a film role that made him a national favourite, and finally a second career as an international star in Hollywood blockbusters and on Netflix. He is best known around the world as Driss, the streetwise young carer in The Intouchables (2011), and as Assane Diop, the gentleman thief in Netflix's Lupin (2021–present) — two very different characters, played more than a decade apart, that together explain why his fame has lasted.

What makes his story unusual is where it started. Sy did not come from a film family or a drama school. He grew up in a working-class housing estate outside Paris, the son of West African immigrants, and he got his first break in radio. Comedy gave him his start, drama gave him his breakthrough, and the combination — warmth, timing, and a screen presence that works in French and in English — is what carried him from Canal+ sketches to the biggest film franchises in the world.

Omar Sy Age and Early Life

Omar Sy was born on 20 January 1978 in Trappes, in the Yvelines department west of Paris, which makes him 48 years old in 2026. He is the fourth of eight children. His mother, Diaratou, is from Mauritania and worked as a house cleaner; his father, Demba, is from Senegal and worked in an auto parts factory after moving from Bakel to France in 1962. Sy is of Fulani origin, and Pulaar was spoken at home. The family visited Senegal every other summer.

He was raised in a habitation à loyer modéré — a subsidised housing estate — in Trappes. It was there, growing up, that he became friends with the comedian and actor Jamel Debbouze and the footballer Nicolas Anelka, two friendships that have lasted into his adult life. Nothing in that start pointed obviously towards acting, but Trappes gave him something his later roles would keep returning to: an ear for how ordinary French people actually speak, and a sense of humour built on it.

Comedy Beginnings — Omar et Fred

Sy began his career after high school in 1996, working at Radio Nova, the Paris radio station, where he met his comedy partner Fred Testot. From radio he moved into television, appearing on Le Cinéma de Jamel on Canal+ with Jamel Debbouze, and then creating his own show, Le Visiophon. The partnership with Testot became the defining act of his early career: as "Omar et Fred" the pair co-hosted Service après-vente des émissions — known as SAV des émissions — a short comedy sketch show on Canal+ that ran from 2005 to 2012 and made them household names in France.

Alongside the sketches he built a steady list of small film and voice roles — a voice part in Disney's Brother Bear, the French voice of a sheep, and later the Michelin Man and other characters in the Oscar-winning short Logorama (2009). He also worked with directors Éric Toledano and Olivier Nakache on Nos jours heureux (2006) and Tellement proches (2009), and appeared in Jean-Pierre Jeunet's Micmacs (2009). He also appeared in the television game show Fort Boyard in 2006. It was solid, popular work, but it was still comedy work. The role that changed everything was a drama.

The Intouchables — the Breakthrough

In 2011, Sy starred in The Intouchables (Intouchables), directed by Éric Toledano and Olivier Nakache, playing Bakary "Driss" Bassari, a streetwise young man from the Paris suburbs who becomes the personal care assistant to a wealthy quadriplegic, Philippe, played by François Cluzet. The film was a phenomenon: it topped the French box office in 2011 and became the bestselling French film of all time in its home country, selling more than 19 million tickets.

The role changed Sy's standing overnight. He was voted the third favourite French personality in a national poll, behind Yannick Noah and Zinedine Zidane, and on 30 December 2012 he was voted the favourite personality in France outright, in a poll by Le Journal du Dimanche. For The Intouchables he won the César Award for Best Actor in 2012 — the first Black actor ever to win France's top film prize — along with the Lumière Award and the Globe de Cristal for Best Actor, and a Best Actor award shared with Cluzet at the Tokyo International Film Festival. The film also earned him a Satellite Award nomination and a European Film Award nomination. It remains the performance he is most identified with in France, and the one that opened every door that followed.

Hollywood — X-Men, Jurassic World and More

After The Intouchables, Sy moved deliberately into bigger, international productions. In 2014 he appeared in X-Men: Days of Future Past as Bishop, and starred in Samba, reuniting with Toledano and Nakache. In 2015 he joined the Jurassic Park franchise as Barry in Jurassic World, a role he returned to in Jurassic World Dominion (2022), and played a chef alongside Bradley Cooper in Burnt.

The run continued through the decade: Two Is a Family (Demain tout commence, 2016), in which he played Samuel, a man who suddenly has to raise a baby daughter, was a major hit in France; Chocolat (2016), in which he played the 19th-century clown Chocolat, earned him another César nomination for Best Actor; and he appeared in Ron Howard's Inferno (2016), voiced Hot Rod in Transformers: The Last Knight (2017), and starred in The Call of the Wild (2020) opposite Harrison Ford. In 2022 he starred in the historical drama Father & Soldier (Tirailleurs) as Bakary Diallo, a father who joins the French army in World War I to protect his son — a film about the African soldiers who fought for France. More recent work includes The Book of Clarence (2023) as Barabbas, John Woo's The Killer (2024), the action film Shadow Force (2025) and the French romantic comedy French Lover (2025). An untitled Ocean's Eleven prequel, in which he is cast, is filming for 2027.

Lupin — Netflix Stardom

In 2021, Sy starred in Netflix's Lupin, a modern retelling of the Arsène Lupin stories by French writer Maurice Leblanc, playing Assane Diop, a gentleman thief who models himself on the fictional master thief. The series became one of the most-watched non-English-language series in Netflix's history, and it gave Sy a second global breakthrough — this time as a television lead, and this time in a role that plays directly to his strengths: charm, intelligence and timing.

The success brought a new kind of recognition. In 2021 he was included in the Time 100, Time magazine's annual list of the most influential people in the world. He was nominated for a Golden Globe for Best Actor in a Television Series – Drama for Lupin in 2022, won the Satellite Award for Best Actor in a Drama / Genre Series the same year, and won the Rose d'Or Performance of the Year in 2021. He has since signed a multi-year feature film deal with Netflix and a first-look deal with HBO Max to develop series for multiple markets, especially French-speaking ones. Lupin is still running, and it is the role most international viewers now know him by.

Awards and Recognition

His awards, in one place: César Award for Best Actor (The Intouchables, 2012); Lumière Award for Best Actor (2012); Globe de Cristal for Best Actor (2012); Best Actor at the Tokyo International Film Festival, shared with François Cluzet (2011); Étoiles d'Or for Best Male Newcomer (2012); Virtuoso Award at the Santa Barbara International Film Festival (2013); Globe de Cristal for Best Actor (Chocolat, 2017); Career Achievement Award at the Montreal International Black Film Festival (2021); Rose d'Or Performance of the Year (Lupin, 2021); and the Satellite Award for Best Actor in a Drama / Genre Series (Lupin, 2022). He was also nominated for a Golden Globe (2022), a European Film Award (2012) and a second César for Best Actor (Chocolat, 2017).

Omar Sy Net Worth 2026

Omar Sy's net worth is estimated at between $10 million and $20 million. That range is the honest answer, and it needs explaining. No audited figure for his wealth exists — he has never published his finances, and no Forbes-style valuation is available. Different celebrity-finance sites publish very different numbers: some put the figure around $10 million, others around $20 million, and European sources have quoted €16–20 million, which is roughly the same upper range in dollars.

What is clear is where the money comes from: leading-role fees from French and international film, a Netflix series lead in Lupin plus a multi-year Netflix film deal, voice work in major franchises, and in 2025 a business step — becoming a co-owner of the French basketball club Paris Basketball, alongside David Kahn. A star who can work in French cinema, Hollywood blockbusters and global streaming at the same time commands more than a star who can work in one market alone. Omar Sy Wife and Personal Life

Omar Sy is married. On 5 July 2007 he married Hélène Sy, in Tremblay-sur-Mauldre, after the couple had been together for ten years. They have five children together. Hélène runs CéKeDuBonheur, a nonprofit that supports children's wards at French hospitals, and Siyah Organics, a Sénégalo-American organic food supplements company.

The family lived in Montfort-l'Amaury, west of Paris, before moving to Los Angeles in 2012, when his Hollywood career began. After the move he worked with a language tutor every day and watched Keeping Up with the Kardashians to improve his English — a detail he has told interviewers himself. He is a Muslim, and a supporter of Olympique de Marseille. In 2023 he signed the Artists4Ceasefire open letter, and he is an ambassador for the 2026 Summer Youth Olympics in Dakar, Senegal — a role that connects his public life back to the West African roots of his family.
